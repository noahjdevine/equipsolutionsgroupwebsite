Agriculture & hard-to-find parts

The counter says discontinued. The number superseded twice and the third one is on backorder. That is where this desk earns its keep.

Send whatever you have — a number stamped in a casting, a photo of the broken piece, or just the make, model and what it does. Old iron is worth keeping in the field, and most of it is still findable if someone is willing to make the calls.

  • Tractor and implement parts across common and obsolete lines.
  • Superseded and discontinued numbers traced to a current equivalent.
  • Aftermarket alternatives when OEM is gone — with an honest note on quality.
  • Wear parts and consumables sourced in quantity for the season.
  • Hydraulics, bearings, seals and drivelines matched by dimension when the number is unreadable.

  • Machine make, model and serial. Serial numbers resolve most number conflicts on their own.
  • Part number, if legible. Include every character you can read, even partial.
  • Photos. The part, where it mounts, and any stamped or cast markings.
  • Quantity and urgency. Down machine, scheduled maintenance, or stocking up.
