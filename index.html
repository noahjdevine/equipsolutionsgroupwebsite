How sourcing works

Why an independent rep

The factory builds it. I make sure you get the right one.

Specs on a website are a starting point, not a spec'd truck. Chassis wheelbase, CA dimension, body length, hook height, PTO and pump selection, subframe, controls, upfit shop scheduling — that is where orders go wrong, and that is the part I do with you before anything gets built.

  • Application first. What you haul, how often, and on what chassis — before we talk model numbers.
  • Honest fit. If a smaller hoist or a different line does the job better, I will tell you.
  • One thread. Quote, order, build, delivery and the first parts call all go through the same person.
  • After the sale. Parts, manuals, warranty paperwork and troubleshooting do not become someone else's problem.

Process

From first call to a unit that works

Four steps, in this order, every time. The sequence matters — skipping the first one is how people end up with a hoist that will not clear their body.

01

Talk it through

Application, volumes, chassis, budget and timeline. Fifteen minutes on the phone usually settles more than a week of emails.

02

Spec the unit

Model, capacity, body length, hook height, subframe and options matched to your truck and your work — with the trade-offs named out loud.

03

Written quote

Pricing, lead time, freight and upfit. No moving numbers. If something changes upstream, you hear it from me first.

04

Build and back-up

Order placed, build tracked, delivery coordinated. Then parts, manuals and warranty support for as long as you run it.

Who I work with

Fleets, contractors and dealers

The equipment is different but the problem is the same: keeping a truck earning instead of sitting.

  • Roll-off and dumpster operators — adding hooklift capacity or replacing an aging cable hoist.
  • Municipal and government fleets — one chassis running plow, dump, flatbed and container bodies across the year.
  • Concrete and paving contractors — short pours, remote sites, and jobs where ready-mix waste and wait time eat the margin.
  • Farms, ranches and implement dealers — parts hunts that dead-end at the counter.
  • Equipment dealers and upfitters — a rep who answers the phone during a build, not three days later.
