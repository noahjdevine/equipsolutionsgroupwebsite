How volumetric differs

The clock does not start until you pour

A drum truck leaves the plant with concrete already mixing and a hard deadline attached. A volumetric plant carries sand, stone, cement, water and admixture in separate compartments and mixes continuously at the discharge. Nothing sets on the way to site.

  • Pour what you need. Order by the job, not by the truckload. No paying for waste, no short-load fees.
  • Change the mix mid-load. Different strengths or slumps from the same load, adjusted in minutes.
  • No return concrete. Unused material stays dry in the bins and goes back to the yard as inventory.
  • Reload while running. The cascade feed lets you top up hoppers during production without stopping the pour.
  • Small wash-down. Only the mixing section contacts wet concrete, so cleanup uses a fraction of the water.

After delivery

A plant is only as good as the operator

Volumetric equipment rewards a trained operator and punishes a guessing one. Calibration, gate settings and flowmeter positions are what stand between a good pour and a rejected one.

  • Operator training. Batching, calibration, mix design and concrete technology — not just which lever does what.
  • Startup checks. Gates calibrated to the mix design, flowmeters set, cement hopper clutch selected, water and admixture switched to automatic.
  • Maintenance and rebuild. Full unit restoration and service through the manufacturer network.
  • Genuine SMI parts. Stocked components engineered for the unit, many cross-compatible with other volumetric brands.
  • Bilingual support. English and Spanish, on the phone and on site.
